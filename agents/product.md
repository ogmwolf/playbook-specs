# product.md — Playbook Product

## The problem

Parents of youth athletes managing college recruiting hold critical information in too many places — texts, emails, notes apps, spreadsheets, Hudl links, and memory. No single place to see what's happening, what needs to happen, and whether they're on track.

## The solution

Playbook is a private family command center. One place for everything related to athlete development and recruiting. Not a recruiting platform. Not a social network. A personal operating system for one family.

## User 1

The developer's wife. Managing twin 16-year-old boys — Jake (Libero) and Wyatt (Setter/Opp) — both playing club volleyball (SCVA) and high school volleyball (Mira Costa HS). Class of 2028. Early in the DI recruiting process.

Her pain: recruiting information scattered across texts, notes, Hudl, emails, and memory. No single place to see what's next or who needs what.

Her job to be done: open the app, see what needs to happen this week, feel in control.

She currently uses ChatGPT to ask recruiting questions — typing in Jake's full context every time she wants advice. The Advisor feature (v2.1) will eliminate that re-explanation by having the AI already know her athletes.

## Primary persona

Busy mom. Non-technical. Managing 1–3 athletes across 1–2 sports. Phone is primary device. Has 60 seconds to check in. Needs to feel organized and in control.

## Core value props (priority order)

1. Time savings — see what to do in under 10 seconds
2. Organization — everything in one place
3. Peace of mind — nothing sitting in a text thread
4. Recruiting advantage — organized families move faster

## What Playbook is not

- Not a recruiting platform (no coach-facing tools)
- Not a social network (no profiles, follows, feed)
- Not a stats analytics tool
- Not a team management tool
- Not public

## Design principles

Simple over feature-rich. Task-first. Mobile-first. Private by default. Multi-athlete and multi-sport by architecture. Value on day one.
